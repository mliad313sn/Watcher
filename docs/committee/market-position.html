Design & Adoption Committee · Session 03 — Market Position

Where Watcher wins,
and what it must close

The committee assessed Watcher against the nine products buyers actually shortlist — Datadog, Zabbix, Checkmk, PRTG, OpManager, LogicMonitor, Nagios XI, the Grafana/Prometheus stack, and SolarWinds — grounded in current (2025–26) pricing and capability research. The verdict, the capability matrix, the honest gap list, and the sharpened positioning follow.

Watcher is the modern, self-hosted console for teams who outgrew Nagios but can't — or won't — move to SaaS observability. It drops into an existing Nagios estate, folds alert floods into ranked root causes through built-in dependency correlation, and ships on-call, runbooks, and a public status page in the box. No PagerDuty. No Statuspage. No per-host meter. Nothing that phones home.

02

The pluses — and the honest gaps

What makes Watcher better than the field today, and what a buyer will fairly hold against it. Gaps marked CLOSED shipped during this session.

ADVANTAGES Where Watcher beats the field

1
Correlation-first, as a primitive. Automatic topology root-cause + child suppression is core architecture. Datadog and LogicMonitor sell it as an upgrade; Zabbix/Checkmk/Nagios XI make you hand-author it.
2
Drop-in for the Nagios installed base. Same engine, same plugins, same checks — with a console from this decade. The largest legacy estate in monitoring becomes the funnel.
3
The incident stack is in the box. On-call rotations + escalation + runbooks + status page with zero add-on cost. The market sells this as three products (PagerDuty + Statuspage + docs). Grafana's OnCall OSS is now archived — timing favours us.
4
Air-gap credible. Fully self-hosted, vendored fonts, no CDN, no phone-home. OT/ICS, defense and regulated buyers are structurally excluded from Datadog/LogicMonitor.
5
Predictable cost. No per-sensor (PRTG), per-host (Datadog), or per-node (Nagios XI) meter, and no 3-year subscription lock-in (SolarWinds).
6
Operator-grade UX. ⌘K palette with actions, guided tour, light/dark, one-tap mobile ack with no app install, bulk ack, assignment. A visible generation ahead of Zabbix/Nagios XI/Icinga.

GAPS What buyers will hold against us

CLOSED
Teams & PagerDuty channels. Shipped this session: native Teams MessageCards and PagerDuty Events v2 (deduped per alert), joining Slack/webhook/email.
CLOSED
Light theme, tour, bulk ops. Daylight theme + onboarding tour + bulk ack + assignment + saved views — the demo-to-daily-use parity set.
P0
SSO — SAML / OIDC / LDAP. The single biggest deal-blocker left, and it gates the exact regulated buyers we target. Committed next.
P1
SLA reports & scheduled exports. Availability reporting exists; PDF/CSV scheduling to rival Nagios XI Enterprise/OpManager does not yet.
P1
Deeper auto-discovery & agents. CIDR discovery exists; L2/LLDP auto-topology and an OS metrics agent trail Zabbix/Checkmk/PRTG.
P2
Anomaly detection, logs, cloud APIs. Deliberately out of the wedge. We say so plainly rather than pretend — full-stack observability buyers should buy Datadog or Grafana.
03

The three buyers Watcher wins

Positioning is choosing. Watcher does not chase cloud-native APM buyers — it wins these three segments decisively.

SEGMENT A

Nagios shops modernizing

Thousands of estates still run Nagios Core/XI or Icinga. Watcher keeps their engine, plugins and checks, and replaces the console, alert flood and bolt-on sprawl in one move.

DISPLACES Nagios XI · Icinga — same engine, modern everything else
SEGMENT B

Air-gapped & regulated ops

OT/ICS, defense, government, healthcare networks where SaaS is disqualified by policy. They still deserve correlation, on-call and a status page — nobody else pairs air-gap with that set.

BEATS Zabbix · Checkmk Raw on integrated incident tooling; Datadog/LM can't bid
SEGMENT C

Mid-market NOCs & MSPs

Priced out of Datadog, burned by PRTG sensor math and SolarWinds lock-in. Multi-tenant from day one, flat predictable cost, and the ⌘K console operators actually enjoy.

BEATS PRTG · SolarWinds on cost model; OpManager on incident stack
